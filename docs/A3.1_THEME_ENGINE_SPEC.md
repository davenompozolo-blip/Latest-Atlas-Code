# A3.1_THEME_ENGINE_SPEC.md

Handoff spec for CC. Completes A3 and authorises the detection engine.

Project: `vdmojjszvvcithuxwexx`. Requires A0b and A3.0.

All four driver classes are now specified. **Every threshold below is authored, not
derived** — seeded at `logic_version = 'v0-uncalibrated'` for exactly that reason. §5's
historical backfill is what turns them into calibrated numbers, and it is the deliverable
that matters most in this unit.

---

## 1. Two amendments to A3.0

**Add `absorbed` to the `regime_theme_states.state` CHECK.** A shock that happened and did
not rebase is a real outcome, not an absence of one, and it should be recorded as such
rather than left as `dormant`.

Full state set: `dormant | emerging | established | absorbed | exhausted | aborted`.

**Extend the operator CHECK** on `regime_theme_triggers` to:
`gte | lte | abs_gte | abs_lte | pct_gte | retrace_gt`

`abs_lte` is what lets a trigger require that something *did not* move, which is how the
attribution rules in §4 are expressed.

## 2. `fiscal_dominance` — seeded

**The claim:** sovereign issuance and industrial policy are setting the price of duration,
not inflation. The signature is **nominal yields rising while inflation expectations stay
put** — a real-yield, bond-supply story — with the long end leading, so the curve bear
steepens.

`driver_class = 'fiscal_dominance'`

| role | series/axis | operator | threshold | units | baseline | hold | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| emergence | `DGS10` | `gte` | 40 | bp | 60 | 30 | nominal long end rising |
| emergence | `T5YIFR` | `abs_lte` | 12 | bp | 60 | 30 | **and inflation expectations flat — this is what makes it fiscal rather than inflationary** |
| emergence | `T10Y2Y` | `gte` | 25 | bp | 60 | 30 | bear steepening: long end leads |
| absorption | `DGS10` | `gte` | 40 | bp | 60 | 20 | rates up… |
| absorption | `T10Y2Y` | `lte` | 0 | bp | 60 | 20 | …but curve flat or flattening — that is monetary tightening, not fiscal supply |
| abort | `DGS10` | `retrace_gt` | 60 | pct | — | — | |
| exhaustion | `T10Y2Y` | `retrace_gt` | 50 | pct | — | — | slope gives back its steepening while the level holds |
| confirmation | `T10Y2Y` | `gte` | 15 | bp | 60 | — | slope, not an axis — see §4 |

Reasoning on the numbers: 40bp over 30 sessions on `DGS10` is roughly 1.5σ of a 30-session
move, loose enough to catch 2023's issuance-driven repricing and tight enough to exclude
ordinary drift. The 12bp `abs_lte` band on `T5YIFR` is the discriminator and is the most
important row here — widen it and this theme starts co-firing with tariff rebasing.

**State honestly in `notes` that this is the weakest of the four.** It has no independent
macro series: nonresidential fixed investment is quarterly and lags by months, so the theme

detectable in a quarter — but this theme is the one most likely to be recalibrated or
retired after the backfill.

## 4. Attribution — the rule that stops manufactured agreement

Four themes; all four would move `T5YIFR`, `DGS10` or `cyclical` if each were assessed
alone. The discriminating variable set is **{`T5YIFR` move, `BRENT` move, `T10Y2Y` move,
`concentration` level}**, and the `abs_lte` rows above encode it.

| | `T5YIFR` | `BRENT` | `T10Y2Y` | `concentration` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `tariff_rebasing` | **up** | flat | — | — |
| `energy_dislocation` | **up** | **up** | — | — |
| `fiscal_dominance` | **flat** | — | **up** | — |
| `productivity_capex` | **flat** | — | — | **up** |

Two requirements on the engine:

**Emergence requires every `emergence` row for that theme to hold simultaneously**, each for
its own `hold_sessions`. Not any, not a majority — all. A theme whose negative condition
(`abs_lte`) fails has not emerged, however strong its positive conditions are.

**Add `tariff_rebasing` emergence row:** `BRENT` `abs_lte` 15 pct, baseline 120, hold 20.
A0b's `BRENT` did not exist when A3.0 seeded that theme, so the negative leg is missing and
tariff would currently co-fire with every energy shock.

**Confirmation is not gating.** A theme can emerge without confirmation. Record confirmation
pass/fail in `evidence` and reflect it in `strength`; never let it block a state transition.
A theme detected on macro data that fails ratio confirmation is a genuinely interesting
state and the engine must be able to report it.

## 5. The engine

### State machine
```
dormant   → emerging      all emergence rows hold, each for its hold_sessions
emerging  → established   emergence rows continue to hold a further 40 sessions
dormant   → absorbed      all absorption rows hold
any       → aborted       any abort row fires
established → exhausted   any exhaustion row fires
aborted / absorbed / exhausted → dormant   60 sessions with no emergence row holding
```

Evaluate once per session, per theme, per `logic_version`. Write one
`regime_theme_states` row every session for every theme, including `dormant` — a gap in the
series must mean the job did not run, never that nothing happened.

Write a `regime_theme_transitions` row only on change.

### `strength`
Mean over that theme's emergence rows of `min(1, actual / threshold)`, then multiplied by
the fraction of `hold_sessions` satisfied. Range 0–1. Record the components in `evidence`,
not just the result.

### `evidence` jsonb
Per trigger: `trigger_key`, `role`, observed value, threshold, baseline value, sessions
held, pass/fail. The state must be fully re-derivable from `evidence` without re-querying
the series. This is what makes a shadow result auditable three months from now.

### Nightly
`cron.job` only, 23:30 UTC Mon–Sat, after the 23:10 factor refresh. Gate on both the A0b
macro load and the factor refresh having succeeded for the run date; if either has not, exit
non-200 and close `sync_log` as `skipped` with the reason. No-op answers non-200.

**Shadow mode: no UI, no API route, no component.** Nothing reads these tables yet.

## 6. Historical backfill — the deliverable to examine

Run the engine over all available history and report what it found. This is the point of the
unit: it converts authored thresholds into calibrated ones in days rather than in the 90
forward sessions shadow mode would take.

Window: from the latest inception among each theme's required series. `productivity_capex`
needs the axes, so 2007+; the other three reach further.

**Report, per theme:**
1. Every transition with its date, from-state, to-state and strength.
2. Total sessions in each state, and the share of history spent `dormant`.
3. Longest `established` run, and its dates.
4. Count of `absorbed` episodes versus `emerging` ones.

**Then check it against what actually happened.** These are the periods the framework was
built to catch, and each is a pass/fail on the calibration:

| Expected | Theme | If it does not fire |
|---|---|---|
| 2018–19 trade war | `tariff_rebasing` | thresholds too tight, or `BRENT` negative leg too strict |
| 2022 energy shock | `energy_dislocation` | 25%/120-session pairing wrong |
| 2025 tariff rebasing | `tariff_rebasing` | as above |
| 2023 issuance repricing | `fiscal_dominance` | `T5YIFR` band too narrow |
| 2023–2025 AI concentration | `productivity_capex` | 60-session holds too long, or theme not viable |

**A theme that fires in none of its expected periods, or fires in most periods, is
miscalibrated — report it as such rather than adjusting the thresholds to fit.** Changing a
number is a `v1` row set, authorised separately, after I have seen the `v0` output. Do not
tune and re-run silently.

### Reproducibility
Emit a digest per run into `sync_log.details`: an md5 over
`(theme_key, state, round(strength, 6))` across all themes, ordered by `theme_key`.
**Do not hash rounded floats to compare series** — that failed in A0 for known reasons; this
digest covers one session's discrete states, where it is safe.

Report the transition counts per theme from the backfill, then re-run the backfill from
scratch and confirm the counts reproduce exactly. That is the checksum, and it is the thing
that makes the `v0` numbers a baseline rather than an anecdote.
